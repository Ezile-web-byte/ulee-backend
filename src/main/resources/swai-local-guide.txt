LOCAL GUIDE (curated background knowledge; treat as general guidance, not exact data)

NELSON MANDELA UNIVERSITY (NMU) CAMPUSES IN GQEBERHA (PORT ELIZABETH)
- South Campus: main campus in Summerstrand, close to the beach.
- North Campus: in Summerstrand, close to South Campus.
- Ocean Sciences Campus: in Summerstrand, near the seafront.
- Second Avenue Campus: in Newton Park.
- Bird Street Campus: in Central (the city centre area).
- Missionvale Campus: in Missionvale, north-west of the city centre.
Students often live in Summerstrand (walking distance to South and North campuses), Humewood, Walmer, Newton Park, Central, Richmond Hill and Mount Pleasant, and travel by walking, minibus taxi, Algoa Bus, ride-hailing, or their own car.

AREAS AND TYPICAL STUDENT LIFE
- Summerstrand: student hub. Beach, restaurants, cafes, pubs and the Boardwalk Mall and casino complex are on the seafront.
- Humewood: beachfront next to Summerstrand, Bayworld and Pollok/Humewood beaches, a bit quieter.
- Walmer: residential, Walmer Park Shopping Centre.
- Newton Park: residential and shopping, Greenacres Shopping Centre, close to the Second Avenue Campus.
- Central and Richmond Hill: city centre, restaurants and nightlife, Bird Street Campus.

SHOPPING
- Boardwalk Mall (Summerstrand, seafront), Greenacres Shopping Centre (Newton Park), Walmer Park Shopping Centre (Walmer), Baywest Mall (west side of the city, needs transport).
- Groceries, pharmacies and banks are usually found inside these malls, so living near one makes daily shopping easier.

STORES, FOOD AND HANGOUTS (what students use day to day)
- Boardwalk Mall (Summerstrand, on the seafront): has a Spar for groceries, a Spur for sit-down meals and celebrating with friends, and a McDonald's for a quick, cheap meal. It sits next to the casino complex (adults 18+ only) and the beachfront, so it is the main hangout for students living in Summerstrand and Humewood.
- Greenacres (Newton Park), Walmer Park (Walmer) and Baywest Mall (west side): large malls with supermarkets, clothing stores, pharmacies, banks and food courts. Students living in Newton Park, Walmer and Central usually shop at these.
- Well-known South African chains such as McDonald's, KFC, Nando's, Steers, Wimpy and Spur are common in the malls and along the main roads, so cheap food is easy to find in every student area.
- Summerstrand's beachfront also has cafes, restaurants and pubs; Central and Richmond Hill have more restaurants and nightlife.

SOCIAL LIFE, CLUBS AND MEETING FRIENDS
- Running: the Summerstrand and Humewood beachfront promenade and Hobie Beach are popular for runs and walks. Running groups and free weekly parkrun events take place on Saturday mornings in the area; the parkrun website shows the nearest one.
- NMU societies, cultural groups and sport clubs are the easiest way to make friends. Orientation week and the clubs and societies days at the start of the year are the best time to join.
- Weekend ideas with friends: a meal at the Spur or the beachfront restaurants at Boardwalk, swimming or surfing at the beaches, a walk along the promenade, the Route 67 art walk in Central, or a day trip to Sardinia Bay (needs transport).
- Most social life for first-year students happens in Summerstrand because many students live there and can walk between campus, the beach and Boardwalk.

BEACHES AND THINGS TO DO
- Hobie Beach, Pollok Beach and Humewood Beach (swimming and surfing spots; check conditions and lifeguard cover), beachfront promenade for running and walking.
- Bayworld (Humewood), St George's Park, Donkin Reserve and the Route 67 art walk in Central, Sardinia Bay beach and nature reserve (needs transport).
- Student societies, sports clubs and running groups usually meet around the campuses; details change each year.

ARRIVING IN GQEBERHA FOR THE FIRST TIME
- By air: Gqeberha has its own airport, a short drive from the city. Ride-hailing, a pre-booked shuttle or a metered taxi are the usual ways to reach student areas. Agree the price or use the app price before you start the trip.
- By road or long-distance coach: confirm the exact drop-off point with the coach company, then use ride-hailing or a taxi for the last leg. Do not walk with luggage after dark.
- Arrange your arrival time with the landlord before you travel so someone can hand over keys. Try to arrive in daylight.
- On the first day: check the room against the agreement, take photos of any damage, and note where the nearest supermarket and pharmacy are.

GETTING AROUND WITHOUT A CAR
- Walking: realistic for students living in Summerstrand and near South, North and Ocean Sciences campuses. Use well-lit routes and walk in groups after dark.
- Algoa Bus: the city bus service. Routes and timetables change, so check the current route before relying on it.
- Minibus taxis: cheap and widely used, but routes are not always clear to newcomers. Ask a local student which route to take, and avoid them at night.
- Ride-hailing (such as Uber and Bolt) is a common option, especially at night. Prices change with demand.
- Fares, timetables and routes change, so never quote exact prices; say they should check the current price.
- NMU may run shuttles between its campuses; students should check with the university.
- A car is helpful for Baywest Mall, Sardinia Bay and the Missionvale Campus, but many students manage without one by living close to their campus.

CHOOSING WHERE TO LIVE (general guidance)
- Walking distance to campus saves transport money and time, but rooms close to the beach in Summerstrand can cost more.
- Single rooms give the most privacy and quiet for studying. Sharing and commune rooms are usually the cheapest.
- Ask about what the rent includes (wifi, water, electricity) because this differs between listings.
- Think about which campus you attend: Summerstrand suits South, North and Ocean Sciences; Newton Park and Walmer suit the Second Avenue Campus; Central suits Bird Street.

LOAD SHEDDING AND POWER CUTS
- Scheduled power cuts (load shedding) happen in South Africa, including Gqeberha. Schedules change, so students should check the current one.
- Backup power (such as an inverter or generator) and wifi differ between buildings. Only say a listing has backup power if its listed features say so. Otherwise tell the student to ask the landlord.
- Practical tips: keep a power bank, charge devices and laptops before the scheduled outage, and keep a torch handy.

FIRST WEEK CHECKLIST (general)
- Confirm your lease or agreement and what you pay each month.
- Check the room, take photos, and report problems to the landlord straight away.
- Learn your route to campus in daylight and save the numbers of the landlord and the ULEE team.
- Find the nearest supermarket, pharmacy and a safe, well-lit route home.
- Join a society, sports club or running group to meet people. Orientation week at the university is also a good time to make friends.

MEETING PEOPLE AND STUDENT LIFE
- NMU societies, sports clubs and residence-style events are the easiest way to meet people.
- Summerstrand has the busiest student social scene (cafes, restaurants, pubs, beachfront); Central and Richmond Hill have more nightlife.
- Details of clubs and events change each year, so students should check with the university.

SAFETY (general advice)
- Travel in groups after dark, use trusted transport or ride-hailing at night, keep valuables out of sight, and use well-lit routes.
- Do not leave bags or phones visible in cars or on beach walks. Swim only where there is lifeguard cover.
- Tell a friend or flatmate where you are going when you go out at night.

HOW ULEE WORKS
- Students browse approved listings on ULEE, open a listing and use "View & Apply". Payments and bookings happen through ULEE or the landlord, not through this chat.

WHAT THIS GUIDE DOES NOT KNOW (do not guess; tell the student to ask the ULEE team or the landlord)
- Required documents for applying, deposit rules and when deposits are refunded, whether a listing is accredited for NSFAS or other funding, whether parents or sponsors can pay, and exact payment dates.
- Whether visitors, partners, pets or holiday stays are allowed, and what is included in the rent for a particular listing.
- Exact walking times, distances and prices of transport. Give only rough estimates and suggest Google Maps for the exact route.
- Pier 14 and other listing addresses: do not describe what is around them unless the listing details say so.
- Maintenance contacts, house rules, and viewing arrangements for a specific listing.
